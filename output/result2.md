## A silent climate crisis on a titanic scale

### ELECTRONICS: A GROWING CLIMATE THREAT
- The electronics sector is responsible for up to 4% of global carbon emissions.
- That's more than aviation – and growing faster. By 2040, electronics could contribute up to 14% of global emissions.
- If the sector were a country, it would be the 5th largest emitter on Earth – after China, the U.S., India, and Russia.

### PCBS: THE UBIQUITOUS, UNSUSTAINABLE BACKBONE
- PCBs are in every electronic device, from smartphones to satellites.
- Nothing digital exists without them.
- The $97B global PCB market is growing fast and is still built on high-emission legacy materials.
- PCB manufacturing emits up to 20 kg of CO₂ per square meter, and scales with billions of devices.

### E-WASTE: A CRISIS BUILT ON PCBS
- The world generated ~67 million tonnes of e-waste in 2024.
- That's the weight of 957 cruise ships – less than 23% is recycled.
- The PCB is one of the most toxic and least recyclable components of this waste stream.

# THE GO-TO-MARKET STRATEGY

## TIER 2 - Target key value chain decision-makers
Focus on securing our place on the BOM, ensuring recurring orders.

1. **OEMs (Original Equipment Manufacturers) + The Decision Makers**  
OEMs like Bosch, Siemens, Webasto specify materials on the Bill of Materials (BOM). Soluboard® must be listed here to ensure its use across all production channels.

2. **CEMs (Contract Electronics Manufacturers) + The Manufacturers**  
CEMs (e.g., Jabil, Flex, Foxconn) manufacture devices on behalf of OEMs. They follow the BOM exactly - if Soluboard® is specified, they must use it.

3. **PCB Fabricators + The Material Buyers**  
PCB fabricators source raw materials per the BOM. If Soluboard® is on the spec sheet, they are required to procure it for production.
